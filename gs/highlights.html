Self-assessment: strengths and challenges

Strengths

Turning messy situations into structured problems. Whether it was a battery's fixed charging window at Mindsets, a bootcamp budget with uncertain attendance, or alliances under an electoral law, my instinct is to ask what the decisions, constraints and goal are. Then I build something that can be tested.

Leading under pressure. Coordinating support for displaced families, and serving as Supply Chain Manager in a case competition, showed me I can organize people and resources when time is short and the stakes are real.

Challenges I am working on

Programming depth. I came to Python later than to Excel, and I still rate myself as building my skills. My FYP forecasting work and my research are deliberately pushing me to write, test and document code to a professional standard.

Accepting what I cannot control. In the FYP, our partner's data arrived late, and there was nothing I could do to speed it up. What I learned was to keep moving with public data and a labelled stand-in, and to be transparent about what each result is based on. I want to bring that calm, honest approach to every project.

Looking ahead

Across my FYP, internship and projects, one thread keeps appearing: making good decisions when the future is uncertain. I saw it in forecasting restaurant demand, scheduling a battery against volatile prices, building election alliances when voter support is unknown, and keeping medicines available in a crisis.

After graduating in May 2027, I plan to pursue a master's degree focused on operations research and AI-driven decision-making. I want to deepen my skills in robust and stochastic optimization and machine learning, then apply them in supply chains, energy and the public sector, especially in places like Lebanon where good planning makes a real difference.
